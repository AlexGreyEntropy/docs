## Role in the vault

<Term
  word="perpetual hedge"
  part="noun"
  phonetic="per · pech · oo · ul · hej"
  example="When SOL dropped, the perpetual hedge booked the move as profit."
>
  A short SOL perpetual position sized against your deposit. It cancels price exposure and earns funding and downside moves.
</Term>

The hedge is what makes a Thaler vault delta-neutral. It is a short SOL perpetual sized against your principal deposit, so a move in SOL's price does not on its own change the value of what you put in.

It also earns, in two ways:

| Source | When it pays |
|--------|--------------|
| Funding | When longs pay shorts, which on SOL has been most of the time |
| Downside capture | Every time SOL falls, the move is booked as profit |

## Funding

<Term
  word="funding rate"
  part="noun"
  phonetic="fun · ding · rayt"
  example="Longs paid the funding rate to shorts for most of the month."
>
  The payment that longs and shorts exchange on a perpetual to keep its price close to spot. When it is positive, the short side gets paid.
</Term>

On the live vaults, funding paid the short 65 to 70 % of hours held in July 2026, and more than 85 % of the time from mid August to mid September. Funding can turn negative, but it has done so less than about 20 % of the time. It is a stream that mostly runs toward the vault, not one that only ever does.

## Downside capture

When SOL falls, the short is in profit. The engine closes it to realise that profit, which settles in USDC, swaps the USDC back to SOL, and reopens the short at its base size at the new lower price. The effect is like dollar-cost averaging into more SOL while the price falls.

This is the part of the strategy that earns most exactly when staking, lending and other long-based yields earn least. In the four weeks before launch, the team's own vaults returned over 15 % in SOL, most of it during a sharp decline.

## Holding through rallies

A rally is when a short is under pressure. Two things keep it safe:

1. **SOL is the collateral.** The short is margined in SOL, so as SOL rises the dollar value of that margin rises too.
2. **Extra margin on demand.** If the liquidation buffer thins, the engine borrows extra SOL from the lending loop's headroom and posts it as margin. When the short is later reset, that SOL is repaid.

The hedge has held through SOL rallies from $65 to $84 and from $74 to $110 without a liquidation. See [Track record](/overview/track-record#stress-events).

## Why a perpetual

A perpetual never expires, so the vault can hold it for its whole life without rolling it. Its funding adjusts continuously and pays every funding period rather than at a single settlement.

## When funding compresses

As perpetual markets mature and more people run the same hedge, funding is likely to compress. It does not disappear: people still pay a premium to be long, and there are still arbitrage opportunities. Downside capture does not depend on funding at all, and the staking and lending legs keep earning either way.
