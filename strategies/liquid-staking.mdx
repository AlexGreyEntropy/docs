## Role in the vault

<Term
  word="liquid staking token"
  part="noun"
  phonetic="lik · wid · stay · king · toh · ken"
  example="The vault holds a liquid staking token that keeps earning staking rewards."
>
  A token that stands for staked SOL plus its rewards, and that can still be moved and used. Also called an LST. jitoSOL and mSOL are two examples.
</Term>

Every Thaler vault starts with a liquid staking position. Your SOL is staked into an LST, which earns Solana staking rewards through its exchange rate. Because the token can still be moved, the vault can supply it to a lending market while the staking reward keeps accruing underneath.

Without liquid staking, the lending loop and the hedge would not be possible.

## Supported tokens

## How a token is chosen

You do not pick the token. [Pulse Router](/overview/architecture#pulse-router) compares the supported LSTs and routes the vault to the one with the best available yield, within the policy. Each token has different characteristics and yield at any given time, and having nine to choose from makes the strategy more adaptable.

## How rewards are realised

Staking rewards are paid into the LST's exchange rate. Over time the token is worth more SOL than when the vault received it. When the vault closes, the LST is converted back to SOL at the current rate, and that growth is part of your return.

## Provider risk

The staking leg inherits the record of the chosen provider. Thaler does not run its own validators. If a provider's contracts fail or its token depegs badly, the vault is exposed to that loss. A severe LST depeg is also one of the few events that could force the lending loop to unwind. See [Risk disclosure](/security/risk-disclosure).
